## Ransomware Tabletop Exercise – Session 2 Notes

### Exercise progression

It was reported that more than 20 users interacted with the suspicious email, and one physician completed the full action associated with the malicious content. The discussion focused on diagnosing the compromise, determining the blast radius, isolating affected systems, maintaining clinical operations, and escalating the incident.

## Initial containment and enterprise response

When malicious activity is suspected, the team would contact Infrastructure and Operations to restrict connectivity. Actions could include:

* Terminating active VPN sessions.
* Disabling VPN tunneling.
* Applying Conditional Access restrictions.
* Isolating affected network segments or VLANs.
* Determining the overall blast radius before restoring connectivity.

Kevin was identified as the primary person capable of performing network-level isolation. A trained backup resource has not yet been confirmed and is needed.

PAG would remain accessible as the backup clinical access method if the primary environment or network connection had to be restricted.

## Training and downtime readiness

Managers would be responsible for directing their teams to use the appropriate downtime tools and procedures.

Security-awareness training is currently provided through Delta Dental for both employees and contractors. A more formal Emerge-specific training process is still being developed. Ownership for ensuring that training is assigned, completed, and tracked still needs clarification.

# Scenario 2.1 – Suspected endpoint infection

### Scenario

Dr. Richard opened a file received through email. After opening the file, the workstation became slow and files could no longer be opened.

### Initial response

David Pye would lead the initial endpoint investigation and work with Trevor when Information Security assistance is required.

The immediate containment step would be to disconnect the affected workstation from the network. A Jira process exists for documenting the incident, but the group noted that immediate communication and coordination must occur before waiting for normal ticket processing.

### Diagnosing ransomware

Possible indicators discussed included:

* High CPU utilization.
* High memory utilization.
* Files becoming inaccessible.
* Unexpected file or folder modifications.
* Microsoft Defender alerts.
* Endpoint or device telemetry showing unusual activity.

David and Trevor would use Defender and available endpoint telemetry to review device events and determine whether the activity is consistent with ransomware.

The group noted that there is currently no separate tool dedicated specifically to identifying folder modifications. Device telemetry would be used for this purpose. Deep Freeze was mentioned during the discussion, but no applicable folder-monitoring solution was confirmed.

### Reimaging and recovery

A suspicious or compromised device may need to be reimaged. However, the team should first:

1. Isolate the device.
2. Preserve relevant logs and evidence.
3. Investigate the activity.
4. Confirm that a replacement device or backup access method is available.
5. Reimage or replace the device after the required evidence has been collected.

The team emphasized investigating before formally declaring the event to be ransomware. At the same time, containment should not be delayed while the final classification is being determined.

The issue would receive the highest operational priority and should be addressed immediately.

### Vendor-related risk

Some systems and recovery capabilities depend on external vendors. The group discussed the need to “trust but verify” vendor capabilities.

A formal third-party risk management process was not identified during the meeting. This represents a potential gap, particularly for vendors that support critical systems, backups, recovery, or clinical operations.

# Scenario 2.2 – Confirmed ransomware

### Scenario

After troubleshooting, the team confirms that the workstation is infected with ransomware. Files are encrypted, and additional files appear to be actively encrypted.

### Immediate containment

The confirmed affected system should be logically isolated and physically or virtually disconnected from the network.

Information Security would coordinate the response, with assistance from onsite technical staff where physical access to the device is required.

Additional workstations are available and could be provided to affected users so that operations can continue while the infected device is investigated or rebuilt.

### Monitoring the spread

The team would use available real-time indicators, Microsoft Defender information, logs, and endpoint telemetry to monitor for signs that the ransomware has spread to other systems.

The following would need to be investigated:

* Other devices communicating with the affected endpoint.
* Similar file-encryption activity on other systems.
* Suspicious login or authentication activity.
* Unusual network traffic.
* Access to shared folders or network drives.
* Evidence of lateral movement.
* Other users who interacted with the original email.

The expected detection and response time was discussed, but a defined response-time target was not confirmed.

### Stakeholder notification

Mark would be the first senior stakeholder notified after the incident is validated or reaches the required escalation threshold.

A war room would then be established to coordinate technical response, business continuity, clinical operations, communications, and leadership decisions.

# Scenario 3 – Possible widespread compromise

### Scenario

At least one device is confirmed to be affected by ransomware, and the team suspects that multiple systems may also be compromised.

### Response steps

The team would:

1. Logically isolate confirmed affected systems.
2. Disconnect affected devices from the network.
3. Investigate for lateral movement.
4. Disable VPN tunnels if there is a risk of propagation through connected environments.
5. Escalate the incident to leadership and other required stakeholders.
6. Isolate affected network subsections or VLANs.
7. Move clinical operations to downtime procedures or PAG where needed.

Any VLAN can be disabled when necessary to contain the incident. Kevin is currently trained to perform this action.

### Clinical and business continuity

Backups are available for patient data. The discussion did not fully confirm the following:

* When the backups were last tested.
* How quickly patient data could be restored.
* Who authorizes restoration.
* Whether backups are isolated from the production environment.
* Whether ransomware could reach the backup environment.

Downtime procedures are available for appointments and other in-progress clinical activities.

Revenue Cycle Management processing was described as automatic, although the expected behavior during a prolonged outage should be confirmed.

PAG would remain available as an alternate access path where the primary environment is unavailable.

### Communications

Possible communication and coordination methods discussed included conference bridges and Confluence pages.

The formal communication plan should define:

* Who starts the conference bridge or war room.
* Who creates and maintains the incident page.
* Who provides updates to employees and clinics.
* Update frequency.
* Approved communication templates.
* Which communication channels remain available if normal systems are affected.

### External resources

No dedicated cybersecurity response team or outside incident-response resource was identified during the discussion.

Mark maintains contact information for other departments that may need to participate. However, the complete contact and escalation list should be documented and made available outside the affected production environment.

# Key decisions captured

* Suspected affected devices should be disconnected from the network immediately.
* David Pye would lead endpoint troubleshooting, with Trevor engaged when security support is needed.
* Defender and endpoint telemetry would be used to determine whether activity is consistent with ransomware.
* Confirmed ransomware requires logical isolation of the device and investigation of the potential blast radius.

* PAG and downtime procedures would support continuity of clinical operations.

# Gaps and risks identified

1. No confirmed backup resource for Kevin’s network-isolation responsibilities.
2. No clearly documented ransomware diagnosis and containment procedure.
3. Unclear ownership for tracking security-awareness training.
4. No defined response-time objective for identifying and containing ransomware spread.
5. No dedicated tool confirmed for monitoring file and folder changes.
6. No formal third-party risk management process identified.
7. Backup restoration testing, ownership, isolation, and recovery timing require confirmation.
8. No dedicated internal cybersecurity response team was identified.
9. No outside incident-response or forensic support arrangement was identified.
10. The complete stakeholder contact list and escalation process are not centrally documented.
11. The incident communication process, channels, templates, and ownership need to be formalized.
12. The relationship between Jira documentation and immediate emergency escalation needs to be documented.

## Tracker-ready summary

During the second ransomware tabletop session, the team evaluated a scenario in which more than 20 users interacted with a malicious email and one physician fully opened the associated content. The response would begin with immediate isolation of the affected device, investigation by David Pye with support from Trevor, and review of Microsoft Defender alerts and endpoint telemetry. If ransomware is confirmed, Information Security and onsite technical staff would isolate the system, investigate the blast radius, monitor for lateral movement, and provide a replacement workstation. If multiple systems or connected environments are at risk, I&O may terminate VPN sessions, disable VPN tunneling, apply Conditional Access restrictions, and isolate affected VLANs. Kevin is currently the primary resource for network isolation, but a trained backup is needed. Mark would be notified first, and a war room would be established for broader coordination. PAG, patient-data backups, and existing downtime procedures would support clinical continuity. Key gaps include the absence of a documented ransomware procedure, unclear training ownership, no confirmed backup network resource, limited third-party risk management, unverified backup recovery details, and no identified dedicated internal or external cybersecurity response capability.
